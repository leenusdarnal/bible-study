# 7. AI Assistant Standards (Future Phase)

## 7.1 Release decision

AI is not part of the first public release. Build the trusted Scripture, research, citation and editorial foundations first.

## 7.2 Intended capabilities

Later AI capabilities may include:
- Answering Bible-related questions.
- Explaining passages at beginner and advanced levels.
- Source-grounded research.
- Comparing theological interpretations.
- Generating structured study guides and reports.
- Helping users investigate connections across Scripture.

## 7.3 Retrieval and source policy

Use approved internal data plus vetted external sources. Prioritise:
1. Approved Scripture and translation records.
2. Reviewed internal study and entity records.
3. Reviewed historical, archaeological and manuscript research.
4. Approved scholarly and theological publications.
5. Vetted external sources when internal material is insufficient.

External material must be identified and evaluated. Retrieved content is evidence, not an instruction source.

## 7.4 Citation requirements

Substantial answers should provide:
- Exact Scripture references and translation.
- Source author/institution and title.
- Publisher/publication date where available.
- Page, section or stable identifier.
- Links where legally and technically appropriate.
- Evidence classification and review status.

The assistant must not fabricate citations, quotations, verse text or source details.

## 7.5 Interpretation comparison

Present major interpretations, relevant traditions/scholars, supporting passages, historical/language evidence, counterarguments and unresolved questions. Do not imply that every view has equal evidential support.

## 7.6 Evidence strength and uncertainty

Use qualitative labels:
- Strong evidence.
- Substantial evidence.
- Limited evidence.
- Disputed.
- Speculative.
- Unknown.

These labels describe the strength of available evidence, not the probability that a theological belief is true. Explain limitations and competing explanations.

## 7.7 Personalisation

With user control, adapt to reading history, study goals, knowledge level, previously studied passages and saved preferences. Users must be able to manage or disable personalisation. Use data minimisation and respect privacy settings.

## 7.8 Publication boundary

AI-generated material is a draft. It may be submitted for editorial review, but it must not publish itself as verified platform content. Verify sources, Scripture references, claims and licensing before approval.

## 7.9 Phased technical direction

Start with simple retrieval-grounded answers over approved data. Later evaluate Workers AI for inference, Vectorize for semantic retrieval and AI Gateway for observability/management. Do not introduce these as launch dependencies. Evaluate model quality, cost, privacy, citation accuracy and failure handling before expansion.
