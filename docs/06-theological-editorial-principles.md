# 6. Theological and Editorial Principles

## 6.1 Platform identity

Bible Study Repo is Scripture-centred and combines biblical study, historical research and comparative theology.

Protestant traditions are the primary focus. Catholic, Orthodox and other Christian traditions should still be represented accurately where relevant to church history, canon, theology and comparative study.

The platform is not intended to erase differences or present one tradition's interpretation as universally accepted.

## 6.2 Interpretive methods

Present different methods and explain when they are useful:
- Historical: historical setting and context.
- Literary: genre, structure and literary features.
- Grammatical: vocabulary, syntax and language.
- Contextual: immediate and wider textual context.
- Canonical: a passage within the whole canon.
- Biblical-theological: development of themes across Scripture.
- Theological: doctrinal meaning and implications.
- Comparative: differences across traditions and scholars.

Do not apply one method mechanically to every passage. Explain methodological assumptions and limitations.

## 6.3 Difficult and disputed passages

A substantial study should consider:
1. Passage and immediate context.
2. Historical and cultural setting.
3. Literary genre and structure.
4. Original-language evidence where available.
5. Relevant textual variants.
6. Major interpretations and who holds them.
7. Biblical and historical support.
8. Counterarguments and difficulties.
9. Unresolved questions and limitations.

## 6.4 Chronology and historical questions

Present multiple chronology models when credible alternatives exist. Record dates/ranges, assumptions, biblical and external evidence, scholarly positions, conflicts and uncertainty.

Do not force disputed dates into a single definitive timeline. Clearly distinguish dates stated in a source from dates inferred by a chronology model.

## 6.5 Apologetics and criticism

Present apologetic arguments and critical objections fairly. Include the claim, arguments, evidence, responses, counter-responses and remaining limitations. Avoid straw-manning or presenting weak arguments as representative of an entire tradition or discipline.

## 6.6 Evidence categories

Distinguish:
- Explicit biblical statement.
- Textual or manuscript evidence.
- Historical/archaeological evidence.
- Scholarly conclusion.
- Theological interpretation.
- Inference.
- Disputed claim.
- Speculation.

A historical source may contextualise a biblical passage without independently proving every detail in it.

## 6.7 Editorial tone

- Respectful and precise.
- Clear for beginners without oversimplifying.
- Detailed enough for advanced readers.
- Transparent about uncertainty.
- No fabricated sources, quotations, manuscript readings or archaeological claims.
- Clearly attribute positions to the relevant authors, traditions or schools.
