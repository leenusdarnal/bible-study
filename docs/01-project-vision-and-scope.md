# 1. Project Vision and Scope

## 1.1 Vision

Bible Study Repo is a comprehensive, interconnected Bible learning, study and research platform. It should help people understand Scripture, learn how to study independently, investigate biblical history and explore connections across the biblical canon.

The platform should serve complete beginners as well as experienced readers, teachers, pastors and advanced researchers.

**Guiding principle: simple at first, powerful when needed.**

## 1.2 Core objectives

1. Make Scripture accessible through a clear and customisable reader.
2. Teach people how to study biblical passages independently.
3. Connect passages, people, places, events, themes and historical contexts.
4. Provide a trustworthy research environment with transparent sources.
5. Present theological and scholarly differences fairly.
6. Support personal study, learning progress and offline access.
7. Build a foundation for a source-grounded AI research assistant in a later phase.

## 1.3 Canon and translations

- Initial canon: Protestant 66 books (39 Old Testament, 27 New Testament).
- Data architecture must support additional canons later, including Catholic and Orthodox canons.
- Target translations: NIV, KJV and NKJV.
- The first release will use legally distributable text.
- NIV and NKJV text must not be redistributed until the appropriate permissions are obtained.
- KJV rights must be checked for the specific edition and jurisdiction; do not assume every edition or use is unrestricted.
- Translation display should be configurable, including single translation, switching and parallel comparison when the relevant texts are licensed.
- Translation-specific versification differences must be mapped explicitly.

## 1.4 Audiences

- Beginners who need accessible explanations and guided learning.
- Regular Bible readers who need reading, notes and cross-references.
- Teachers, pastors and study leaders.
- Researchers investigating textual, historical, archaeological and theological questions.

## 1.5 First-release scope

The initial public release is a complete basic platform, not the entire long-term vision.

- Complete 66-book Scripture framework.
- Legally distributable translation text.
- Bible reader and search.
- Book, chapter and passage study material, expanded progressively.
- Encyclopedia foundations for biblical people, places and events.
- Scripture cross-references.
- Initial maps and timelines.
- Personal library: notes, bookmarks, highlights and reading progress.
- Optional accounts; public content remains available without registration.
- Responsive design and offline reading foundation.

AI is excluded from the first release.

## 1.6 Long-term capabilities

- Comprehensive study content for every book and passage.
- Additional translations and canons when rights and data are available.
- Original-language Hebrew, Aramaic and Greek tools.
- Knowledge graph, geography, timelines and genealogies.
- Research centre covering history, archaeology, manuscripts, textual criticism, canon and church history.
- Study Academy, courses, quizzes and reading plans.
- Audio, text-to-speech, video and multimedia.
- Source-grounded AI assistant and advanced research workflows.
- Full offline reading and personal study with background synchronisation.

## 1.7 Non-goals for the first release

- No AI assistant at launch.
- No requirement to finish comprehensive study content for all 66 books before launch.
- No redistribution of translations without verified rights.
- No attempt to resolve every theological or chronological disagreement into one answer.
- No microservices architecture before there is a demonstrated need.

## 1.8 Product principles

- Scripture-centred and evidence-conscious.
- Beginner-friendly without limiting advanced research.
- Open public access to public knowledge.
- Personal data and study records remain under user control.
- Provenance and licensing are first-class data.
- Disputed claims are labelled, not hidden.
- Incremental releases must remain usable and testable.
